Exact Reversible Integer Training Without Activation Caching

Scale-preserving integer adjoints and per-layer updates in a bit-exact reversible network, with sequence-learning experiments

Jeong Seong Yeol · South Korea · kdp7584@gmail.com
Research draft, revision 3 · 9 September 2026

Abstract

We train networks whose weights, states and adjoints are integers, whose intermediate states are reconstructed bit-exactly from deterministic additive couplings so that no activation tape is stored, and whose parameters are updated layer by layer during the reconstruction so that no full-depth gradient buffer exists either. Gradient scale survives depth because each adjoint block carries one shared exponent: on the digits benchmark this change alone, with everything else fixed, turns a depth-16 collapse (13.6 ± 2.6%) into 72.3 ± 5.8% over five seeds, and the final 8-bit-weight configuration holds 87% at depths 8, 16 and 32 while the reconstructed activation working set stays at 1.49 MB against a 27.3 MB cached tape at depth 32, at 1.9–2.0× the cached backward's time. Grouped couplings with a dispersing permutation keep 78.7 ± 2.0% on Fashion-MNIST against 79.0 ± 1.3% for dense integer couplings at one eighth of the coupling parameters and multiply-accumulates. Two sequence-learning studies support trainability: a Japanese→Korean model with integer base-2 attention memorises 512 training pairs with the unchanged integer optimizer once momentum is enabled (472/512 greedy exact reproductions; routing the source pathway to momentum 0 reaches the pre-set criterion in half the optimizer steps), and on 66,853 training pairs the model reaches 26.0% validation token accuracy, with integer averaging of three neighbouring checkpoints raising held-out chrF from 7.08 to 7.86 on the selection rows and from 7.86 to 8.42 on rows not used for selection, against 9.8 for a float model with the same objective. A diagnostic that had suggested noisy integer gradients is retracted; the cause — saturation of 32-bit backward accumulators at mantissas wider than 16 bits — is identified. Translation quality is not established by any model here; the sequence results are finite-set learning and validation-selected generalisation measurements, reported with their controls.

1. Contributions, scope and prior work

The central claim is architectural: in a network that keeps integer semantics throughout, combining bit-exact additive reconstruction with scale-preserving block-floating adjoints and per-layer updates removes the activation tape and the full-depth gradient buffer, and the resulting integer surrogate gradients train the network. Everything else in the paper — grouped compaction, the sequence extension, the optimizer experiments, checkpoint averaging — supports that claim or bounds it.

1.1 Relationship to prior work, and what is added

Additive reversible couplings come from NICE and RevNet [1, 2]; exact fixed-point reversal in recurrent networks from [4]; integer training with shared-scale gradients from WAGE, integer mixed-precision training and NITI [6–8, 11]; channel mixing across groups from ShuffleNet [12]. Two neighbours are closest. NITI [8] trains with integer-only arithmetic and per-layer block exponents but stores activations for backpropagation as any non-reversible network does; this paper removes that storage and shows the block-floating adjoint is what keeps a reversible integer stack trainable at depth (Section 4.1). Zhang et al. [5] obtain bit-exact reversibility of transformer activations without changing the architecture, in floating point; here the forward, the reconstruction, the adjoints and the optimizer are all integer-valued, the reconstruction is exact by construction of the modular coupling rather than by a numerical correction, and the layer update is scheduled inside the reconstruction so that the full-depth gradient buffer is also absent. The grouped compaction with a constructive dispersing permutation, the integer base-2 attention with a row-coupled surrogate adjoint, and the optimizer findings are, to our knowledge, not in those works. No priority claim is made beyond the tested combination.

2. Exact integer reconstruction

2.1 Additive coupling

Split an integer state into v = [a, b]. With integer parameter matrices U and V, form a deterministic coupling function f from shifted matrix products and a ReLU:

z = Qt(aUᵀ),   h = max(z, 0),   f(a) = Qt(hVᵀ),   F(a, b) = [a, b + f(a)] mod 2bs

Qt applies a per-row power-of-two right shift chosen from the row's magnitude bit length so the result fits t + 2 bits (t = 5, seven-bit outputs, in every configuration below). Bit lengths are computed with integer shifts and comparisons. A fixed permutation exchanges and mixes the state halves between couplings. The inverse is the same computation with a subtraction:

F−1(a, b) = [a, b − f(a)] mod 2bs

Subtraction exactly undoes addition in the fixed-width modular state space, so f itself need not be invertible; it must only be recomputable — parameters, masks, external encoder memory and any stochastic choices must match the forward evaluation.

2.2 Reconstruction and update ordering

The backward first undoes the inter-layer permutation, then recomputes the local coupling intermediates and recovers b. The fused implementation reuses those intermediates to form the parameter gradients and the input adjoint. Only after these old-parameter computations are complete is that layer updated. This ordering removes the full-depth gradient buffer in addition to the activation tape; the per-layer step is bit-identical to a batched step over all parameters (gate 4, every architecture). Shared parameters or global gradient-dependent optimizer operations would need additional scheduling; the tested architectures and BlockSGD do not.

2.3 Exactness is not overflow tolerance

State wraparound preserves the additive inverse but can destroy accuracy: at 8-bit states on digits, reconstruction stays exact while 7.7% of state elements wrap and accuracy falls to 12.4% (Section 4.3). The inverse guarantee says nothing about matrix-product accumulators, and Section 12 shows a case where 32-bit backward accumulators silently saturate. Product-reduction bounds and deterministic arithmetic semantics remain necessary at every stage.

3. Shared-exponent adjoints and updates

3.1 Preserve scale through depth

The backward represents an adjoint tensor by an integer mantissa m and one exponent e, interpreted as m·2e. A forward right shift s changes the adjoint scale through e ← e − s; mantissa renormalisation transfers its shift into the exponent; addition aligns exponents before combining mantissas. Discarding this metadata changes the relative scale of propagated and residual gradients and destroys deep training (Figure 2). The representation is block floating point [8, 11], not a new number format. A tensor-wide exponent outperformed a per-sample exponent at K = 32 (84.1 ± 2.4% versus 61.0 ± 25.0%); the measured per-sample exponent spread at initialisation was only 1–2 bits, so the finer granularity bought nothing and cost stability.

3.2 Loss and integer error feedback

The image experiments and the sequence memorisation runs use a multiclass margin surrogate: when the margin is violated, the output gradient is +1 on the largest wrong logit and −1 on the true logit. The 67k runs use an integer base-2 cross-entropy head instead (Section 13). BlockSGD block-normalises each parameter gradient's mantissa to 12 bits, accumulates it, and emits a clipped unit move when the accumulator crosses 2shift, keeping the un-emitted residual:

gn  = renorm(gm, 12)                 # per-tensor block normalisation; the exponent e is not used here
acc -= gn
q   = clamp(sign(acc) · (|acc| >> shift), -1, +1)
w  += q;  w = clamp(w, -2^(wb-1), 2^(wb-1) - 1)
acc -= q << shift                    # shift = 11 + lr_bits; lr_bits >= 1 keeps |acc| < 2^shift

The shared exponent is essential during propagation; the parameter update then uses its own block normalisation and deliberately does not treat the absolute exponent as a learning rate. The step is a sign-type update with error feedback, not a real-valued SGD step.

3.3 Momentum, as implemented

BlockSGD's momentum option (momentum_bits = k) is an integer exponential average inserted before the accumulator, shift-only and per parameter, so per-layer and batched stepping remain equivalent:

m -= rshift0(m, k);  m += gn;  gn = renorm(m, 12)     # decay 1 - 2^-k per step

With k = 7 the decay is 127/128 per step: after 128 steps (eight epochs of the 512-pair task at batch 32) a fraction (127/128)128 ≈ 0.37 of any initial buffer content remains; after 300 steps about 0.10. Split momentum routes each parameter tensor to one of two canonical BlockSGD instances: target-side tensors (target embedding and positional table, decoder self-attention, decoder FFN, output head) step with k = 7, and the source pathway (source embedding and positional table, encoder blocks, decoder cross-attention) with k = 0. No optimizer code is duplicated; a gate over 48 real training steps on the 512-pair model (282,755 weight moves) confirms that routing with k = 7 on both sides is bit-identical to the uniform optimizer in all 25 parameter tensors.

4. Depth, grouping and image accuracy

4.1 Controlled depth failure

On digits, changing only the backward representation rescues K = 16: the legacy mantissa-only backward reaches 13.6 ± 2.6%, the shared-exponent backward 72.3 ± 5.8%, and the five per-seed pairs do not overlap (Figure 2b). At K = 8 the legacy backward is still adequate (86.9 ± 3.8% versus 68.7 ± 8.8%), so this is a depth-specific rescue. With the final 8-bit-weight, 8-bit-gradient and 12-bit-state configuration, K = 8/16/32 reach 87.3 ± 2.2 / 86.7 ± 0.8 / 86.8 ± 1.0%: accuracy no longer depends on depth.

4.2 Compaction and a constructive mixing rule

Retain U and V entries only where input and output indices share a residue class modulo g. Compacting the nonzero entries yields g dense submatrices with parameter and multiply-accumulate counts reduced by 1/g; the coupling stays exactly reversible because f remains deterministic in a. Between couplings the grid-transpose permutation

p(i) = ⌊i / g⌋ + (i mod g) · (M / g)

distributes each old residue group uniformly over the next layer's groups when M/g is divisible by g, combined with the half exchange. Roll and xor controls, which preserve group identity, are substantially worse at narrow per-group widths. Cross-group mixing is necessary in the tested models; the particular permutation is one constructive choice, not the only one.

4.3 State width

For a fixed digits seed, 63-, 12- and 10-bit states give the same 89.33%. At 8 bits accuracy falls to 12.44% with 7.7% of state elements wrapping, while reconstruction stays exact. The inverse guarantee and the empirical accuracy limit are separate facts and are reported separately.

What is constant, and what the memory claim covers. Only the reversible activation working set is constant in depth. In the digits profile, parameters plus optimizer state are 95% of the total at K = 64 once the full-depth gradient buffer is gone. In the sequence model the encoder and decoder cache-free working sets are 2,252,800 and 2,777,088 bytes at K = 2, 4 and 8, while the cached tape grows 3.59× from K = 2 to 8; the 3,644-pair sequence run had a peak process RSS of 611 MB, which includes the Python runtime and workspaces. The reconstructed backward costs 1.92–1.98× the cached backward's wall time in the reference implementation. A comparison against gradient checkpointing at matched total peak memory has not been made and is required before the memory result is quoted as a system advantage rather than a structural property.

What is measured. The implementation uses int64 containers and a float64 GEMM route that is exact under asserted bounds and cross-checked against int64. The arithmetic semantics are integer; the timings say nothing about native int8 kernels, energy or cross-device bit equality.

6.1 Integer base-2 attention and its surrogate

After subtracting the maximum valid score, the forward maps score differences to powers of two: wi = 2rshift0(si − max, 3) + 12, clamped at zero, so a row has at most 13 weight levels. The backward uses competitive row coupling,

∂L/∂si = wi(gi − ḡ),   ḡ = ⌊Σj wjgj / Σj wj⌋,

one integer weighted sum and one integer floor division per row. The production arithmetic is integer-only but not division-free. Against a float row-coupled reference on identical inputs the integer adjoint has cosine 0.99392; the diagonal variant has 0.65094.

6.2 Structural verification

Every new sequence probe in this revision re-ran the full gate set before training: exact inverse, cache-free = cached backward, per-layer = batched stepping, attention adjoint cosine against autograd (ReLU mirror), future-target mutation at cuts 1/3/6, batch/PAD invariance, masked-memory content invariance, and a live check that the coupled adjoint differs from the diagonal one. All passed (9/9) at the d96 sequence configuration. The split-momentum routing was separately verified bit-identical to the canonical optimizer (Section 3.3).

7. Deferred debt on the 8k run: a causal pathology, not a solution

On the 7,600-pair integer run the optimizer failed in a different way, traced in Appendices F–H of the 5 September manuscript. Legal single-coordinate cross-attention moves improved the content margin in 62.9% of 256 probes, yet the unrestricted joint update (a median 1,918 coordinates per batch) worsened it, and decoder cross-attention was the group whose removal most repaired the step. A cap on cross-attention firings suppressed the epoch-40→41 regression; adaptive shadow selection improved the current-batch objective but deferred most cross updates, and the retained error-feedback debt of those deferred firings was shown by an accumulator-only intervention (95% paired confidence interval [+18.17, +20.28] on the source-memory margin) to degrade later source conditioning. The mediator is persistent fired-pool recurrence and starvation (median fired-but-unselected run of 22 steps), not top-rank occupancy. A prespecified TTL16 rule bounds the debt and recovers the 64-step margin, but over a full epoch its train hinge is worse than every comparison (Figure 4). Bounded debt is an established pathology and a suppressible one, not an optimizer.

The 512-pair results below do not rerun this trajectory. Momentum was never enabled in the 8k diagnostics; whether it removes the debt pathology at that scale is untested.

8. The split-momentum study

8.1 Question and configuration

Plain BlockSGD memorises 32, 64 and 128 pairs (32/32, 53/64, 111/128 at d = 96) and had been reported to fail flat at 512 pairs (0/512 after 7,200 steps, teacher-forced content ≤ 0.5%). The question is whether the optimizer's momentum option, and where it is applied, changes that. All 512-pair runs use the historical configuration: d = 96, FFN 192, two encoder and four decoder blocks, learned positional tables of 16 rows, batch 32 (16 steps per epoch), lr_bits 1, 8-bit weights and tables, base-2 attention with shift 3 and cap 12, the coupled adjoint, and the hinge head. The 512 pairs are the historical set (a superset of the 128 set; no duplicate sources or targets, no source with two targets; hash in Appendix A.5). Evaluation every 150 epochs: teacher-forced content accuracy (non-EOS tokens), position-0 accuracy (first target token from the source alone), greedy exact reproductions out of 512, and content accuracy under real, shuffled and zeroed encoder memory on a fixed 64-sentence slice — which is why the real-source probe and the full teacher-forced number differ by a few points.

8.2 The 256-pair control: what momentum does early

Observation. On 256 pairs, where plain BlockSGD learns source conditioning (position-0 12.9% at epoch 150), enabling uniform momentum 7 raises teacher-forced content from 8.3% to 23.0% at the same epoch while position-0 falls to 1.2% and the zeroed-source probe (31.5%) exceeds the real-source probe (22.7%): early in training the model predicts better when the source is removed (Figure 6a). On 512 pairs the same delay appears in the uniform run — position-0 stays below 9% until epoch 450 and reaches 83% at 750 — and disappears when the source pathway is routed to momentum 0 (Figure 7c). Source momentum 3 already loses the effect (position-0 1.95% at epoch 150 versus 14.3% at 0), and target momentum 5 is slower than 7 (greedy 2/512 versus 179/512 at epoch 300).

Hypothesis, not measured. The proposed explanation is that source-pathway gradients of different examples conflict, so their exponential average carries little signal until the shared target structure has been learned, whereas per-batch (momentum-free) updates on those tensors move on each example's own signal. This would account for the delay and for its removal by the split. Neither gradient cancellation across examples nor the per-tensor update alignment has been measured; the uniform run's later takeoff and higher endpoint show that momentum delays source conditioning here rather than preventing it.

11. Small sets: no regression, faster convergence

At 128 pairs split momentum reaches 102/128 by epoch 300 and 124/128 (TF 99.6%, real-source probe 100%) by epoch 900; it exceeds the plain optimizer's best over 3,000 epochs (111/128, at epoch 2,700) by epoch 450. The two runs share model, seed and data and differ only in the optimizer; the plain run is 3.3× longer, and no single “speed-up factor” is quoted because the two curves have different shapes.

12. Correction: the production gradient is unsaturated; wide mantissas are not

An earlier diagnostic compared the production gradient (hidden_bits 16, accum_bits 32) against a “wide reference” at hidden_bits 40 with the same 32-bit accumulators and found encoder cosines of 0.002–0.017. That conclusion was wrong and is retracted: the reference was the broken measurement. The backward routines dense_linear_backward and bmm_backward_shared saturate their matrix products at accum_bits, and the weight-gradient reduction over batch × T rows needs about hb + 16 + log2(rows) bits in the worst case; the normalised operands of the 16-bit path stay inside 32 bits, while at hb ≥ 24 they do not.

Cosine alone does not establish element-level agreement, so three finer statements were measured on two batches at two model states (Appendix A.3, grad_exactness.json). First, the production backward is elementwise identical, mantissa and exponent, to the same 16-bit backward run with 63-bit accumulators: no accumulator ever clips, so the production gradient is exactly what its integer definition specifies. Second, against the 32-bit-mantissa backward the unrounded cosine is 0.999995–0.999999, the 12-bit BlockSGD votes derived from the two agree in sign on 99.72–99.996% of coordinates and in value on 92–95%, and the represented values differ elementwise by a mean relative deviation of 3–18% that is dominated by near-zero elements (maximum relative deviations of 2–3 × 103 occur on elements the reference puts at the bottom of its range). Third, the 16-bit mantissa is not simply the truncated image of the 32-bit one (5–27% of elements): each stage of the 16-bit backward renormalises, so its rounding accumulates through depth. The correct statement is therefore: the production gradient is the exact integer surrogate it is defined to be, and it is a rounded version of the wider surrogate whose direction and update signs it reproduces to well under 0.3%; “exact real gradient” is not claimed. Consequences: the historical hb24/hb28/hb32 runs on 512 pairs were training on clipped gradients (hb24's partial success was a clipping side-effect), any wide-mantissa reference must widen the accumulators, and the saturation counter R.STATS["accum_saturations"] is now reported in every run — it is zero for every result in this paper except where stated.

13. 66,853 training pairs: continuation and integer checkpoint averaging

13.1 Configuration and the float references

The full short-sentence split of the mixed TED/Tatoeba corpus (--max-tok 14 --max-len 16) has 66,853 training and 3,518 validation pairs; the historical “8k” runs used 7,600 of them. The model is d = 192 (4,878,336 parameters), character tokenisation with a 5,000-token vocabulary, batch 32 (2,089 steps per epoch). Three float references on this split fix what the integer model is compared against: float Adam with softmax cross-entropy reaches 33% validation token accuracy and held-out chrF 11.3; the token hinge caps float at 17% / chrF 5.2 whatever the attention; an integer-implementable base-2 cross-entropy with an exact per-row normaliser recovers 28.7% / chrF 9.8. On the integer side the same head ported (pi = 2li − max for classes within 20 of the row maximum, one integer division per row, 24-bit fixed-point p/Z) changed nothing at 8-bit weights, and every int8 run logs a maximum weight magnitude of 128 from epoch 0; with 10-bit weights the same run climbs monotonically (12.5 → 21.6% over 12 epochs), twelve-bit weights are slower per epoch and never catch up, and uniform momentum trails the split.

Hypothesis, not measured. The reading that the int8 weight clamp is the binding constraint is an association (clamped weights present; 10-bit helps) and not a causal test: the number of updates blocked by the clamp, and the interaction of range with step size, have not been measured.

The continuation is an optimizer restart, not a full-state resume. The trainer's --resume restores the weights and the accumulators of the target-side BlockSGD instance. It does not restore the source-side accumulators, either momentum buffer, the data-order generator (the permutation sequence restarts from that of epoch 0, so epochs 14–25 re-use the batch orders of epochs 0–11), the epoch counter, the best-checkpoint state or the patience counter. With decay 127/128 the momentum buffers do not “refill” in 128 steps — 37% of a buffer's content still reflects its initial state after 128 steps and 10% after 300 — so the first epoch after the restart runs on partly zeroed momentum. The gain from epoch 13 to 17 (+3.5 all-token, +5.0 content) may therefore be continued training, a restart effect, or both; the control that separates them — a resume from a complete saved optimizer state versus a restart from the same weights — has not been run. The trainer now saves the full state; the control is the first item of Appendix A.6.

After epoch 18 the run oscillates within a band of 22–26% all-token accuracy. Halving the step rate (lr_bits 2) from the epoch-17 checkpoint reduces the source gap from +6.3 to 0.0 and position-0 from 13% to 3% within one epoch and keeps falling; restarting from an averaged checkpoint at lr_bits 1 returns to the same band (22.9% / 12.6% after one epoch, 22.8% / 10.1% after two). A second averaging cycle does not compound the gain: eight epochs from the epoch-16–18 mean stayed in the band (best single 24.5% / 12.9%), and the mean of that cycle's best ± 1 epoch scores chrF 7.23 on the selection rows and 8.36 on the independent rows — the same per-cycle gain of about +0.7 over its own single checkpoint, but no improvement over the first average (7.86 / 8.42), which remains the best integer model.

Hypothesis, not measured. The band is consistent with a ±1 sawtooth of the sign-type update around a basin the weights cannot settle into, and the lr_bits-2 collapse with the momentum-free source pathway needing the full step rate. Neither the orbit nor the basin has been characterised in weight space; the observations are the band and the two continuations.

13.3 Integer checkpoint averaging

Averaging the weights of neighbouring epoch checkpoints removes most of the oscillation. The rule is integer: for n aligned checkpoints with sum S per weight, the averaged weight is sign(S)·⌊(2|S| + n) / (2n)⌋ (nearest integer, ties away from zero), computed in int64 and written back onto the 10-bit grid; the script avg_ckpts.py is listed in Appendix A.4.

15. Discussion and conclusion

15.1 What is established

  • Bit-exact reconstruction and cache-free, gradient-buffer-free backward with a constant activation working set, for MLP, convolution, attention and encoder–decoder couplings, verified by equality gates at every tested depth, with integer semantics throughout.
  • Shared-exponent adjoints are what makes deep integer training work: a controlled backward-only change turns a depth-16 collapse into normal learning.
  • Grouped couplings with dispersing permutations keep accuracy at 1/8 of the coupling cost; group-preserving mixing does not.
  • The integer sequence system, with the canonical optimizer and momentum enabled, memorises 512 source-conditioned pairs to 92% exact reproduction; split momentum reaches the criterion in half the steps and, at 128 pairs, exceeds the plain optimizer's 3,000-epoch best by epoch 450. Uniform momentum delays source conditioning early (256- and 512-pair observations) and ends higher.
  • Six alternative settings for the 512-pair stall do not help within their budgets; the production gradient is unsaturated and reproduces a wider backward's update signs to 0.3%; the earlier gradient-noise diagnostic is retracted with its cause identified.
  • At 67k pairs the integer model reaches 26% validation token accuracy; integer averaging of adjacent checkpoints gains 0.8 chrF on the selection rows and 0.6 on 800 validation rows never used for selection, while lowering the step rate does not.

15.2 What is not established

  • Translation quality. Every model scores near zero on never-trained sentences at 512 pairs and chrF 7.9 at 67k; the float ceiling on the same data is 11.3.
  • Any of the mechanisms proposed in the hypothesis boxes: gradient cancellation, the weight clamp as binding constraint, the sawtooth orbit, basin mixing.
  • Whether the post-restart gain at 67k is continued training or the restart itself.
  • Multi-seed statistics for sequence results (two seeds agree at 512 pairs; everything else is one trajectory), and an averaging gain on a separate test split (Table 6b uses validation rows outside the selection set, not a test split).
  • A memory comparison against gradient checkpointing at matched peak memory, and native low-bit efficiency: all arithmetic runs in int64 containers with asserted bounds.
  • Whether momentum removes the deferred-debt pathology of the momentum-free 8k run.

15.3 Conclusion

Exact reconstruction, scale-preserving integer adjoints and per-layer updates give activation-cache-free integer training that matches float accuracy on the image tasks within one to two points and holds it to depth 32. The integer sequence model memorises 512 translation pairs with its unchanged optimizer once momentum is enabled, and reaches a quarter of the tokens on a 67k corpus, within 1.9 chrF of the float model that shares its objective. The next controlled steps are the resume-versus-restart control and a second seed at 67k, the averaging gain on an untouched test set, a checkpointing baseline for the memory claim, and the momentum optimizer on the 8k trajectory on which the debt pathology was measured.

out, ins = sys.argv[1], sys.argv[2:]

for ws in zip(*[c["weights"] for c in cks]):

    avg.append(torch.where(s >= 0, (2 * s + n) // (2 * n), -((2 * (-s) + n) // (2 * n))))
base = dict(cks[0]); base["weights"] = avg; base["opt_acc"] = None; base["averaged_from"] = ins
torch.save(base, out)
